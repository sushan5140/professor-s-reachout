**Subject:** Undergraduate research inquiry — when an LLM explanation looks right but is not faithful

Dear Professor Kim,

My name is SUSAN. I finished high school in India this year and I’m preparing to apply for undergraduate study in AI/Computer Science in Korea through GKS.

I found your Data Intelligence Lab while looking through LLM and explainable-AI work, and I was interested in the lab’s current projects around explainable LLM applications.

One question I keep coming back to is whether a good explanation actually reflects what the model used.

I’ve been building Minos-J around the idea of separating a final answer from the evidence or reasoning that supposedly supports it.

For an explainable LLM, I’d like to test something simple: keep the final answer fixed, change or remove one piece of evidence, and see whether the explanation changes in the way we would expect.

If the explanation stays almost identical even after the important evidence is gone, it may be persuasive without being faithful.

I’d start with a small controlled dataset rather than a large benchmark and compare self-explanations against evidence interventions.

I may be oversimplifying explainability research, so I’d really value your opinion on whether this is a useful experiment.

I saw that your lab currently works with an undergraduate researcher. If you think my background could fit, I’d be very happy to start remotely on a small project under your guidance.
