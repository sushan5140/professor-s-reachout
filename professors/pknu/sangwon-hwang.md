**Subject:** Undergraduate research inquiry — detecting shared failure in distributed AI agents

Dear Professor Hwang,

My name is SUSAN. I finished high school in India this year and I’m preparing for undergraduate study in AI/Computer Science in Korea through GKS.

I found AIDAN while looking through multi-agent AI research, and I liked that the lab studies agents inside distributed communication systems rather than only in a simulated reasoning benchmark.

One question came to mind when I was reading through the lab’s direction.

In a distributed system, several agents can agree with each other and still be wrong if they are all working from the same stale or incomplete information. In that case, agreement itself may look like confidence even though the whole group is heading in the wrong direction.

I’ve been building Minos-J around a similar reliability problem in LLM systems, so I wondered whether a small version of that idea could be tested in distributed agents.

I’d start with a simple multi-agent environment, introduce controlled information delay or partial observations, and compare normal consensus signals with the actual decision quality. I’d then test whether an independent uncertainty or disagreement check can identify the cases where agreement is misleading.

I’m still early in communication-network research, so I may be simplifying the distributed-systems side too much. I’d really value your opinion on whether the question is useful.

I saw that AIDAN offers undergraduate research internships. If you think my background could fit, I’d be very happy to start remotely on a small project and learn the networking side while contributing.
