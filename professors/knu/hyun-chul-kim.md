Dear Professor Kim,

My name is SUSAN. I finished high school in India this year, and I’m preparing to apply for undergraduate study in AI/Computer Science in Korea through GKS.

I came across your NeuroAI Lab while looking for groups where the AI problem itself matters as much as the application. I spent some time going through the lab’s recent work, especially the subject-aware fMRI experiments and the work around EEG foundation models.

One thing there felt very close to a problem I have been working on myself.

In a physiological ML project I’ve been doing, I found that a model can look reliable when everyone is evaluated together, but become much less reliable for a particular person. That led me to work on subject-conditional calibration rather than treating every person as if they came from the same distribution.

Seeing your lab work on cross-subject representation learning made me wonder about something slightly different: when a brain foundation model is transferred to a new person, can we measure not only whether its representation transfers, but whether the model actually knows when that transfer is failing?

I was thinking of starting very simply — compare cross-subject performance and uncertainty, identify subjects where confidence and correctness separate, and then test whether a small subject-adaptive calibration step fixes that without retraining the whole model.

I may be missing a much better way to frame this, so I’m not treating it as a finished research proposal. I’d actually value your opinion on whether the question is worth pursuing.

I also noticed that your lab welcomes undergraduate researchers. If you think my background could be useful, I’d be very glad to start remotely and contribute to something already happening in the lab as well.
