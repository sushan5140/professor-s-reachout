**Subject:** Undergraduate research inquiry — testing stability of LLM bias under matched evidence

Dear Professor Shin,

My name is SUSAN. I finished high school in India this year and I’m preparing to apply for undergraduate study in AI/Computer Science in Korea through GKS.

I found DILAB while looking into LLM evaluation, and your work on bias in generated news caught my attention.

The part I found most interesting was not simply that bias appears, but that the same underlying evidence can be framed differently depending on the model’s generation behavior.

That made me wonder about a counterfactual test.

If the evidence and factual content stay fixed but the prompt framing, order of viewpoints, or wording changes, how stable is the model’s stance and quotation behavior?

I’d start with a small matched set where the information is identical and only the presentation changes, then track which outputs change stance, quotation use, or neutrality. I’d be interested in whether the model’s own confidence changes with those surface differences too.

I’m still early in this area, so I may be describing something your existing evaluation already captures better. I’d really value your opinion.
