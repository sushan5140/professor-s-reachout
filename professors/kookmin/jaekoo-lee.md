**Subject:** Undergraduate research inquiry — evaluating hidden failures in generative image editing

Dear Professor Lee,

My name is SUSAN. I finished high school in India this year, and I’m preparing to apply for undergraduate study in AI/Computer Science in Korea through GKS.

I came across your Machine Intelligence Lab and then read about your group’s recent FEAT work on fashion editing and virtual try-on.

What interested me was not only the generation side, but how difficult these systems must be to evaluate.

A generated result can look very convincing at first glance while quietly failing the condition that mattered — changing the wrong region, losing part of the original person, or not really preserving the source design that was supposed to guide the edit.

Most of my own work has been around reliability and evaluation rather than image generation itself, so I started wondering whether there is a useful way to separate “looks good” from “followed the intended edit correctly.”

A small experiment I had in mind would use controlled edits where one property is changed at a time, then compare human or model-based visual-quality scores against separate measurements for identity preservation, edit localization, and condition following. I’d be interested in the cases where the overall quality score stays high even though one of those constraints has clearly failed.

I’m still new to generative vision, so I may be framing the problem too much from an evaluator’s point of view. I’d really value your opinion on whether this is a useful direction.

I saw that your lab currently has intern opportunities. If you think my background could be useful, I’d be very happy to start remotely on a small task and contribute to something already happening in the lab.
