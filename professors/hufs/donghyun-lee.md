Dear Professor Lee,

I’m Susan Reddy Panjugula, an undergraduate applicant from India planning to study Computer Science/AI in Korea through GKS.

I found your recent work on uncovering reliability blind spots through edge-case-based validation, and it is unusually close to the question I’ve been working on myself: when a model has a strong headline metric, what evidence would actually be enough to show that its reliability claim does not hold?

I’m developing a small framework called Meno-J that treats evaluation as a falsification process. It takes a research claim, generates targeted tests against it, and keeps track of what remains supported versus what breaks under specific conditions. Before that, I worked on subject-conditional reliability in wearable physiological ML, where the weakest-person result mattered much more than the aggregate score.

I wondered whether a useful extension of your edge-case validation work could be to make the search for blind spots more iterative — actively generating and prioritizing new edge cases rather than evaluating only a fixed supplementary set.

I’d be glad to start with a narrow remote task: reproducing part of the validation pipeline, implementing an edge-case search component, or testing the idea on a small benchmark. If the direction makes sense, I can send my research CV and a one-page proposal.
