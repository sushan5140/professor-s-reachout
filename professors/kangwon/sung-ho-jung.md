Dear Professor Jung,

My name is SUSAN. I finished high school in India this year, and I’m preparing to apply for undergraduate study in AI/Computer Science in Korea through GKS.

I found your lab while looking through current AI research at Kangwon, and your new multi-agent multi-omics dementia-risk project caught my attention.

I’m still new to the biomedical side, but the multi-agent part connected with a problem I’ve been thinking about in reliability.

If several agents use different biological signals and reach the same prediction, agreement sounds reassuring. But if those inputs are correlated, the agents may agree for the same wrong reason.

In some of my own ML work, I found that an overall confidence number could hide much weaker reliability for one person. That made me wonder whether agreement between agents can sometimes hide a similar problem.

I’d start with a small controlled experiment: remove or perturb one modality at a time and measure whether agent agreement changes in proportion to the actual prediction reliability. I’d be interested in cases where agreement stays high even after an important source of evidence becomes unreliable.

I may be translating a general reliability idea too directly into multi-omics, so I’d really value your opinion on whether the question makes biological sense.

I saw that your lab is currently recruiting undergraduate interns. If you think my AI/evaluation background could fit, I’d be very happy to start remotely on a small task and learn the biomedical side while contributing.
