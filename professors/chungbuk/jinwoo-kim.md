**Subject:** Undergraduate research inquiry — hidden failure cases in VLM safety detection

Dear Professor Kim,

My name is SUSAN. I finished high school in India this year and I’m preparing to apply for undergraduate study in AI/Computer Science in Korea through GKS.

I found CNS Lab while looking into AI security, and HarassGuard caught my attention because it uses a VLM for a problem where a wrong confident decision actually matters.

One thing I kept wondering about was what sits behind the average accuracy.

In my own ML work, I found that a model could look reliable overall while one person or subgroup was much weaker. That made me curious whether a harassment detector can have a similar hidden gap.

For example, the model may work well on clear interactions but become confidently wrong when there is partial occlusion, unusual camera angle, or context that looks similar to harassment without actually being harassment.

I’d start by grouping errors by those conditions, then compare the model’s confidence with the actual failure rate in each group. I’d want to see whether the same VLM can tell us when its own context understanding is weak.

I’m still new to security and social VR, so I may be looking at the problem too much from an evaluation perspective. I’d really value your opinion.
