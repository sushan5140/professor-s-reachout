Dear Professor Nasridinov,

My name is SUSAN. I finished high school in India this year and I’m preparing to apply for undergraduate study in AI/Computer Science in Korea through GKS.

I found your Data Analytics Lab while looking into applied AI, and your recent work on multimodal helmet-compliance detection caught my attention.

What interested me was the fusion itself.

When two models or modalities are combined, the final accuracy can improve even if one input becomes unreliable. That can make the system look robust while one component is quietly failing.

In a physiological ML project I’ve been working on, I found a similar problem at the subject level: the overall result looked good while one part of the data was much less reliable.

I was thinking of a small experiment where one modality is gradually degraded — blur, pose error, partial occlusion — while the other stays clean. I’d compare the final confidence with the true error and measure when the fusion system stops reflecting which source is actually trustworthy.

I’m still learning multimodal systems, so I may be simplifying the fusion behavior too much. I’d really value your opinion on whether this is useful.
