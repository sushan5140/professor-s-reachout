**Subject:** Undergraduate research inquiry — auditing LLM agents for hidden design-rule failures

Dear Professor Seo,

My name is SUSAN. I finished high school in India this year, and I’m preparing to apply for undergraduate study in AI/Computer Science in Korea through GKS.

I found HAI Lab while looking through work on LLM agents, and your recent projects on design-system-compliant interface generation caught my attention.

I’ve been building an independent project called Minos-J where I separate a generator from an auditor whose job is to actively look for reasons the answer may be wrong.

Your UI-generation work made me wonder whether that separation would help with design rules.

An interface generated by an LLM can look completely reasonable to a person while still violating one small rule in the design system — spacing, component choice, hierarchy or interaction behavior. If the same agent generates the interface and then critiques itself, I wonder whether it tends to miss the same rule twice.

A small experiment I’d like to try is to create a set of interfaces with known rule violations, then compare self-critique against an independent auditor that only receives the design rules and rendered result. I’d be interested in which kinds of violations each one misses and whether the independent check is actually worth the extra compute.

I’m not treating this as a finished proposal, and I may be simplifying the HCI side of the problem. I’d really value your opinion on whether this is useful.

I saw that HAI Lab is open to undergraduate and visiting researchers. If you think my background could fit, I’d be very happy to start remotely on a small research task or contribute to something already underway.
