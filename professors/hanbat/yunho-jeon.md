**Subject:** Undergraduate research inquiry — reliability after model lightweighting

Dear Professor Jeon,

My name is SUSAN. I finished high school in India this year and I’m preparing to apply for undergraduate study in AI/Computer Science in Korea through GKS.

I found EffAI Lab while looking into efficient AI, and I liked that the lab works across VLMs, lightweighting, on-device AI and time-series models rather than treating efficiency as a single technique.

One question I keep coming back to is what gets lost when a model becomes smaller.

In my own ML work, I found that an overall score could remain strong while one subgroup became much less reliable. That made me wonder whether compression can create the same kind of hidden failure.

A pruned or quantized model may keep almost the same average accuracy, but confidence or robustness could degrade for a smaller set of difficult examples.

I’d start by keeping the task fixed, applying one lightweighting method at a time, and comparing which examples change from correct to wrong and how their confidence moves. I’d be especially interested in whether the full model already gives an early signal for which samples are most likely to fail after compression.

I’m still early in efficient-model research, so I may be framing this too much from an evaluation angle. I’d really value your opinion.

I saw that EffAI Lab is recruiting undergraduate researchers. If you think my background could fit, I’d be very happy to start remotely on a small task and learn the systems side while contributing.
