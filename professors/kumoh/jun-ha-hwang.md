I’ve been thinking about reliability problems in ML systems, and that made me curious about the failure side of learned heuristics.

If a learned heuristic makes search much faster on the kinds of states it saw during training, what happens when the state distribution changes? I’d be interested in whether it sometimes becomes confidently worse than a simple hand-designed heuristic while still looking strong on the average benchmark.

The small experiment I had in mind would compare a learned heuristic and a standard heuristic under controlled distribution shifts — for example, changing starting-state difficulty or using states farther from the training distribution — and track not only solution quality and search cost, but where the learned heuristic begins to mis-rank states.

I’m still early in optimization and reinforcement learning, so I may be asking something that has a much cleaner formulation already. I’d appreciate your opinion on whether it is a useful direction to explore.
