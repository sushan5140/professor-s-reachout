Dear Professor Jeong,

My name is SUSAN. I finished high school in India this year, and I’m preparing to apply for undergraduate study in AI/Computer Science in Korea through GKS.

I found UNICON Lab while looking into AI and edge computing, and I was interested in your work on UAV/LEO-assisted systems and cloud-versus-edge processing.

One question came to mind from the reliability side.

An offloading policy can be energy-efficient on average, but the decision is being made from estimates of channel quality, sensing requirements, and compute availability. If one of those estimates is slightly wrong, the chosen route may stop being the best one very quickly.

In some of my own ML work, I found that an overall result could stay strong while one subgroup became much less reliable. That made me wonder whether the same thing can happen with offloading decisions.

I’d start with a controlled simulation where only one input estimate is perturbed at a time, then measure when the chosen offloading decision changes from efficient to clearly suboptimal. I’d be interested in whether a simple uncertainty estimate can flag those fragile decisions before execution.

I’m still learning wireless and edge systems, so I may be looking at the problem too much from an ML evaluation angle. I’d really value your opinion on whether it is a useful direction.

I saw that UNICON Lab is currently recruiting undergraduate research interns. If you think my background could fit, I’d be very happy to start remotely on a small project and learn the communication side while contributing.
