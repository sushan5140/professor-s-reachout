**Subject:** Undergraduate research inquiry — confidence when a voice assistant attributes the wrong speaker

Dear Professor Kim,

My name is SUSAN. I finished high school in India this year, and I’m preparing to apply for undergraduate study in AI/Computer Science in Korea through GKS.

I came across SIA Lab while looking into multimodal agents, and your recent work on voice-assistant robustness to third-party interruptions caught my attention.

It made me think about a reliability question that seems easy to miss.

A voice assistant may understand a sentence perfectly but still act on it incorrectly because it attributed the command to the wrong person. In that case the language part is right, but the overall decision is still wrong.

Most of my own work has been around cases where a system looks confident even though one hidden part of the pipeline has failed.

I was thinking of a small experiment where the spoken content stays the same while speaker identity, distance, overlap, and interruption timing are changed. I’d compare the assistant’s final confidence with whether it actually attributed the instruction to the correct speaker.

I’d be especially interested in cases where answer confidence stays high even when speaker grounding is uncertain.

I’m still early in speech AI, so I may be separating the pipeline too simply. I’d really value your opinion on whether this is useful.

I saw that SIA Lab welcomes undergraduate research inquiries, although I also noticed the current capacity note. If you think my background could fit, I’d be very happy to start with a small remote task rather than expecting a formal position immediately.
