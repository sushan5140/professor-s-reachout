**Subject:** Undergraduate research inquiry — detecting unreliable visual world models before action

Dear Professor Seong,

My name is SUSAN. I finished high school in India this year, and I’m preparing to apply for undergraduate study in AI/Computer Science in Korea through GKS.

I found CVLab while looking through Physical AI research, and your current project on action-error prevention through 4D spatiotemporal understanding caught my attention.

The part that interested me was the idea of stopping an error before the action happens.

In a lot of AI systems, the model only gets judged after it has already produced the final prediction or action. I’ve been working on reliability problems where I try to find an earlier signal that something has gone wrong.

For a vision-based physical agent, I started wondering whether the quality of the internal 3D or temporal representation could act as that early signal.

A small experiment I’d like to try is to deliberately remove or corrupt one view or short temporal segment, then measure whether uncertainty in the reconstructed scene increases before action prediction fails.

I’d be interested in the cases where the model still looks confident even though its world representation has already become incomplete.

I’m still early in 3D vision and robotics, so I may be framing this too abstractly. I’d really value your opinion.

I saw that CVLab has been actively working with undergraduate interns this year. If you think my evaluation/reliability background could fit, I’d be very happy to start remotely on a small research task.
